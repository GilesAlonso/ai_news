The Daily Globe: U.S. strikes on Iran continue as Red Sea attacks widen the conflict, U.S. military operations against Iran enter a 13th consecutive night, and U.S. plans delegation to question Brazil’s electoral system

Published on 2026-07-25

World

  • U.S. strikes on Iran continue as Red Sea attacks widen the conflict
    The United States conducted a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iranian targets after Houthi forces attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea; Washington threatened further military action, while Iran responded with attacks on nearby states hosting U.S. bases.
  • ICC member states vote to dismiss prosecutor Karim Khan
    Member states of the International Criminal Court voted to remove prosecutor Karim Khan following allegations of sexual misconduct, according to diplomatic sources cited by Reuters.
  • Spain declares a national emergency as wildfires force evacuations
    Spain declared a national emergency after wildfires near Madrid and in Ávila prompted the evacuation of more than 10,000 people; major fires also led to large-scale evacuations in southern France.

USA

  • U.S. military operations against Iran enter a 13th consecutive night
    The Trump administration continued strikes against Iran and said it was not pursuing talks at that time; the escalation followed Iranian attacks on countries hosting U.S. forces and raised concerns about a wider regional conflict.
  • New U.S. tariffs draw objections from trading partners
    The administration announced new tariffs on dozens of countries and opened a formal investigation into European Union trade practices after the EU fined Google 890 million euros over alleged antitrust violations.
  • Protests follow the fatal police shooting of Corey Ruiz in Madison
    Demonstrators gathered in Madison, Wisconsin, after a police officer shot and killed Corey Durell Ruiz; the family’s attorney called for criminal charges, while officials and investigators continued reviewing the incident.

Brazil

  • U.S. plans delegation to question Brazil’s electoral system
    U.S. officials are expected to travel to Brasília before Brazil’s October 4 presidential election; the Washington Post reported that the mission may raise questions about the integrity of the voting system, while the State Department did not specify its purpose.
  • Brazil rejects U.S. tariffs linked to forced-labor claims
    Brazil’s government called the new U.S. tariffs on its exports arbitrary and unjustified; the trade minister said roughly 2,000 Brazilian products, including coffee and orange juice, would be exempt from the latest measures.
  • Indigenous leaders seek protection from criminal groups in the Amazon
    Asháninka and other Indigenous leaders from the Brazil-Peru border traveled to Brasília seeking government protection, saying organized crime networks are expanding through their territories and contributing to drug trafficking, illegal roads, and forest destruction.
